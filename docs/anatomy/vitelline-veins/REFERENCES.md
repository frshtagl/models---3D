# Vitelline Veins — Scientific Reference Register

**Access date for online sources:** 25 November 2026  
**Scope:** human embryonic venous and hepatic vascular development  
**Citation policy:** cite the exact source supporting each anatomical claim; distinguish primary human reconstruction from review / teaching sources.

## A. Primary and key scientific sources

### 1. Collardeau-Frachon S, Scoazec J-Y (2008)

**Title:** Vascular Development and Differentiation During Human Liver Organogenesis.  
**Journal:** The Anatomical Record. 2008 Jun;291(6):614–627.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ar.20679  
**Full text:** https://anatomypubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ar.20679  
**PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18484606/

**Use in this project:**
- hepatic primordium and vascular chronology;
- early hepatic sinusoidal appearance and proposed precursor mechanisms;
- venae advehentes and venae revehentes;
- sinusoidal endothelial differentiation during later fetal development;
- four intervitelline connections: three prehepatic and one subdiaphragmatic;
- classical afferent / efferent hepatic channels;
- venae advehentes and venae revehentes;
- classical segmental construction of portal trunk;
- subhepatic anastomosis / portal sinus;
- fetal portal-umbilical circulation and ductus venosus;
- hepatic venous outflow and IVC relationships.

**Important limitation:** this is a review / synthesis of earlier embryological work. Its vestigial model should be presented alongside later human 3D reconstruction evidence.

### 2. Hikspoors J P J M, Peeters M M J P, Mekonen H K, Kruepunga N, Mommen G M C, Cornillie P, Köhler S E, Lamers W H (2017)

**Title:** The fate of the vitelline and umbilical veins during the development of the human liver.  
**Journal:** Journal of Anatomy. 2017 Nov;231(5):718–735.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/joa.12671  
**Full text:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/joa.12671  
**PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28786203/  
**PMCID:** PMC5643923  
**PMC full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5643923/

**Use in this project:**
- 3D reconstructions of human embryos from 3 to 10 weeks;
- common hepatocardiac channels;
- vitelline-vein enclosure by hepatoblasts;
- sinusoidal entry from the venous sinus and formation of an intrahepatic network;
- retrograde development of asymmetric draining veins from hepatocardiac channels;
- right-left asymmetry of intrahepatic vitelline conduits;
- dorsal extrahepatic intervitelline connection just caudal to the dorsal pancreatic bud at CS13 and well formed by early CS14;
- smaller, more caudal ventral extrahepatic connection temporarily present during CS14 only;
- left-sided midgut-derived portal precursor;
- portal sinus and venous duct shunt interpretation;
- right versus left intrahepatic portal branch lineage.

**Important limitation:** the paper reports a reconstruction-based interpretation from a finite series of human embryos and comparative animal material. It is important evidence, not an excuse to erase the classical teaching model from the learning experience.

## E. Source hierarchy

When sources differ:
1. Primary human embryo reconstructions and original anatomical studies should be evaluated directly.
2. Peer-reviewed human liver-development reviews provide synthesis and historical models.
3. Clinical pictorial reviews are useful for adult anatomy and classical diagrams.
4. Teaching sites are useful for accessible summaries but must not override more detailed evidence.

Do not claim that source hierarchy alone resolves all disagreement. Record the actual conflicting observations and explain the uncertainty.


### 8. Isotani N, et al. (2024)

**Title:** Regional differences in the umbilical vein and ductus venosus at different stages of normal human development.  
**Journal:** The Anatomical Record. 2024;307(10):3306–3326.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ar.25421  
**Publisher page:** https://anatomypubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ar.25421

**Use in this project:**
- paired early umbilical and vitelline venous context;
- right umbilical-vein regression and persistence of the left umbilical route;
- regional morphology and narrowing of the ductus venosus;
- CS15 reconstruction of the venous route and right, middle and left hepatic veins joining the IVC.

**Limitation:** use the study for the venous-route reconstructions it actually reports; do not infer a complete placental or cardinal venous system from it.

### 9. Hikspoors J P J M, et al. (2015)

**Title:** Development of the human infrahepatic inferior caval and azygos venous systems.  
**Journal:** Journal of Anatomy. 2015 Feb;226(2):113–125.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/joa.12266  
**PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25496171/  
**PMCID:** PMC4304567  
**Full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4304567/

**Use in this project:**
- primary human embryo reconstructions from 4 to 10 weeks;
- caudal cardinal extension from CS11 to CS15;
- subcardinal plexus development in and around the mesonephroi from CS15;
- longitudinal subcardinal channels and intersubcardinal anastomoses from CS16;
- the authors' interpretation of the infrarenal IVC and azygos system as caudal-cardinal derivatives, with renal-region contribution from the subcardinal system;
- explicit comparison with conventional supracardinal / sacrocardinal models.

**Important limitation:** this is a source-specific human reconstruction that challenges conventional venous-system nomenclature. The app keeps it as an alternative interpretation instead of silently replacing the conventional teaching model.

### 10. Imaging of inferior vena cava normal variants, anomalies and pathologies, Part 1: Congenital (2023)

**Journal:** Insights into Imaging.  
**Full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10788309/

**Use in this project:**
- conventional four-segment IVC teaching model;
- hepatic, suprarenal, renal and infrarenal segment mapping;
- conventional right subcardinal, subcardinal–supracardinal and right supracardinal assignments;
- azygos / hemiazygos system context.

**Limitation:** this is a clinical imaging review, not a primary human embryonic reconstruction. It is used to document the conventional textbook interpretation, which is displayed separately from the 2015 human reconstruction.
