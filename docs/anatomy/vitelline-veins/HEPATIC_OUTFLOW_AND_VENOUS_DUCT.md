# Hepatic Outflow and the Venous Duct — Anatomy and Evidence Guide

## Scope

This guide accompanies Milestone 6. It explains the classical efferent hepatic pathway, the human-reconstruction right hepatocardiac drainage, two different ductus-venosus descriptions, terminal hepatic veins and the limited hepatic IVC contribution represented in the 3D workstation.

## 1. Keep afferent and efferent pathways separate

- **Afferent vessels** bring blood into the liver: portal venous branches and the umbilical vein in fetal life.
- **Sinusoids** form the intrahepatic exchange network.
- **Efferent vessels** carry blood away from the sinusoids toward the hepatic veins and IVC.

The portal sinus and ductus venosus are not synonyms:
- the **portal sinus** joins inflow pathways and distributes blood toward the portal / sinusoidal circulation;
- the **ductus venosus** is a shunt that allows part of umbilical venous blood to bypass the hepatic sinusoidal bed and reach the systemic venous outflow.

## 2. Classical efferent pathway — 2008 synthesis

Collardeau-Frachon and Scoazec describe two main early efferent vessels, the primary right and left hepatic veins, receiving blood from the sinusoidal plexus through small channels called **venae revehentes**.

The route is:

1. Hepatic sinusoidal plexus.
2. Venae revehentes.
3. Subdiaphragmatic intervitelline anastomosis above the liver.
4. Terminal primitive vitelline segments.
5. Sinus venosus / venous pole.

The subdiaphragmatic anastomosis joins the cranial right and left vitelline channels. The 3D model shows the bridge and terminal channels as separate selectable layers so the class can follow the route in order.

Do not confuse this cranial subdiaphragmatic connection with the prehepatic portal-trunk anastomoses.

## 3. Human right-sided hepatic drainage — 2017 reconstruction

Hikspoors et al. describe a rightward repositioning of the sinuatrial junction with:
- enlargement of the right-sided venous sinus;
- enlargement of the right hepatocardiac channel;
- narrowing of the left-sided channel;
- a right-dominant hepatic draining-vein network developing retrogradely from the hepatocardiac channel;
- disappearance of the small left hepatocardiac vein and tributaries as distinct channels by late CS14 in their sampled series.

The right hepatocardiac channel is the cranial hepatic outflow route in the human reconstruction. The app labels it as the hepatic / suprahepatic IVC contribution, but does not treat it as the complete IVC.

## 4. Terminal hepatic veins — 2024 human reconstruction

Isotani et al. reconstruct the venous route in human embryos and early fetal specimens. Their CS15 reconstruction identifies right, middle and left hepatic veins joining the IVC.

The app shows three simplified collector paths:
- right hepatic vein;
- middle hepatic vein;
- left hepatic vein.

They converge on the right hepatic outflow junction. Flow arrows point from the liver toward the systemic outflow.

These centre-lines are schematic. They do not reproduce exact specimen coordinates, the full adult branching pattern or Couinaud hepatic segments.

## 5. Ductus venosus — two separate descriptions

### Classical 2008 account

The ductus venosus is a large, branchless vessel connecting the **left angle of the portal sinus** to the IVC. It lies in the prolongation of the umbilical vein but is not described as a direct continuation of the umbilical-vein lumen.

In the model, the classical ductus-venosus path begins at node-left-iva-subhepatic, the left portal-sinus angle, and ends at the hepatic IVC / outflow junction.

### Human 2017 reconstruction

Hikspoors et al. describe a **de novo left-to-right shunt** between:
- the left umbilical vein in the liver hilum; and
- the right hepatocardiac channel.

The app shows this as a separate curved venous duct with a narrowed middle section. It does not start at the classical subhepatic portal-sinus angle.

### 2024 morphology

Isotani et al. describe the human venous route from the umbilical ring through the liver, portal sinus, ductus venosus and IVC. Their reconstructions show regional narrowing and bending of the venous duct, including at CS15. The model's narrowed waist is exaggerated for visibility and is not a measured lumen diameter.

## 6. Portal sinus — a source-comparison caution

The 2017 reconstruction describes a left umbilical-to-right vitelline shunt as the portal sinus. The 2024 study's overview describes portal-sinus connections between the right hepatocardiac channel and the right vitelline vein.

These descriptions should not be silently combined. The current app retains the 2017 portal-sinus geometry and uses the 2024 study for ductus-venosus morphology and terminal hepatic-vein context. A specialist should review the portal-sinus terminology before the app adds another alternative path.

## 7. Postnatal fates

At birth:
- the **umbilical vein** closes and becomes the **ligamentum teres hepatis**;
- the **ductus venosus** closes and becomes the **ligamentum venosum**.

These are two different vessels and two different remnants. The app displays the ductus-venosus postnatal fate in the inspector and explains both in the final teaching step. Separate ligament meshes are deferred.

## 8. IVC boundary — do not overgeneralize

The right hepatocardiac channel contributes to the hepatic / suprahepatic IVC. The complete IVC is a composite vessel assembled from multiple embryonic venous systems.

This milestone does not model the full infrahepatic IVC, renal segment, subcardinal / supracardinal or posterior-cardinal contributions. Do not teach that the entire IVC is vitelline-derived.

## 9. Stage interpretation in the app

| Stage | Teaching interpretation |
|---|---|
| CS10 | Paired vitelline and umbilical systems approach the venous pole |
| CS12 | Sinusoidal network enters the developing liver; early paired venous context |
| CS13 | Classical hepatic efferent channels begin in the teaching sequence |
| CS14 | Human venous duct and classical ductus venosus shown as distinct developing pathways |
| CS15 | Three terminal hepatic veins and the curved, narrowed human venous duct shown in the later early-embryonic bin |
| CS16 | Organized early fetal hepatic inflow and outflow arrangement |

These are broad teaching bins, not exact onset dates for every vessel. The 2024 study includes later stages as well; the current six-stage timeline does not model all of them.

## 10. Coordinate and geometry notes

- +X: embryonic right.
- −X: embryonic left.
- +Y: cranial.
- −Y: caudal.
- +Z: dorsal / posterior.
- −Z: ventral / anterior.

All vessel radii and centre-line coordinates are normalized schematic teaching geometry, not measured embryo diameters or specimen-derived 3D coordinates.
