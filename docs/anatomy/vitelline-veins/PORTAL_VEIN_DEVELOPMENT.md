# Portal Vein Development — Anatomy and Evidence Guide

## Scope

This guide accompanies Milestone 5 of the interactive 3D workstation. It distinguishes the classical three-segment vitelline model from the 2017 human-reconstruction account. Both can be selected in the browser, but they are not combined into one invented developmental sequence.

## 1. Classical portal trunk — three retained components

Collardeau-Frachon and Scoazec describe the classical portal trunk as a composite of three vitelline components. In caudal-to-cranial order:

1. **Left vitelline segment:** from the termination of the mesenteric vein to the middle intervitelline anastomosis.
2. **Middle / retroduodenal intervitelline anastomosis:** the dorsal connection crossing behind the duodenum.
3. **Right vitelline segment:** between the middle / retroduodenal and subhepatic anastomoses.

The other segments of the right and left vitelline veins collapse in this classical account. The portal trunk ends at the right angle of the subhepatic anastomosis.

The app highlights these three components with one gold overlay called **Classical portal trunk**. This overlay is a composite path assembled from the three named structures; it is not a fourth vessel.

## 2. Human reconstruction — midgut precursor and right vitelline continuation

Hikspoors et al. report a small left-sided vein arising from the developing midgut mesentery and draining into the left vitelline vein just upstream of the dorsal intervitelline anastomosis.

The dorsal extrahepatic anastomosis provides the route by which the portal vein crosses behind the duodenum. The right vitelline conduit remains identifiable and contributes to the right-sided portal pathway.

The browser overlay **Human portal pathway** shows:
- a short midgut-derived precursor;
- entry into the left vitelline trunk;
- the dorsal intervitelline crossing;
- the right extrahepatic vitelline continuation;
- the right intrahepatic vitelline / portal continuation.

The source notes that the origin of the more upstream extrahepatic portal vein is poorly documented, although the small left-sided midgut-derived vein is clearly documented in the reconstruction. The 3D overlay is therefore a teaching synthesis of the described topology, not a claim of a fully traced, continuous portal trunk in every specimen.

## 3. Portal sinus — do not conflate the two mechanisms

### Classical 2008 synthesis

The subhepatic intervitelline anastomosis transforms into the portal sinus (sinus intermedius). The review proposes regression of its left half and enlargement of the remaining part. The left umbilical vein terminates at the left angle of the subhepatic anastomosis, while the portal trunk reaches its right angle. The model therefore highlights the subhepatic bridge itself and the left umbilical approach to its left end.

### Human 2017 reconstruction

The portal sinus is described as a de novo intrahepatic left-to-right shunt from the left umbilical vein in the liver hilum to the right vitelline (portal) vein. In this account the left vitelline conduit was already regressing when the shunt developed. The portal sinus persists as the transverse part of the left portal vein.

The app uses two separate selectable layers:
- classical-portal-sinus
- human-portal-sinus

The classical path highlights the left-to-right subhepatic bridge and the left umbilical approach to its left angle. The human path ends on the right intrahepatic vitelline conduit. The two layers never appear together.

## 4. Intrahepatic portal branches

### Human reconstruction

Portal branches become identifiable at CS14:
- right-sided branches arise from the intrahepatic right vitelline (portal) vein;
- left-sided branches in the ventromedial and left dorsolateral liver arise from the left umbilical vein;
- left-sided branches are described in a spiraling arrangement.

The gallbladder marks the boundary / hepatic vascular midline (line of Cantlie) between the right vitelline-derived and left umbilical-derived portal branches.

The app shows a small number of first-order paths to make the origins visible. It does not depict the complete intrahepatic branching tree or Couinaud segmental anatomy.

### Classical synthesis

The 2008 review describes portal branches emerging from the portal trunk and portal-sinus tributaries. It identifies the definitive right portal vein as a remnant of the right vitelline vein, while the left portal vein derives from the portal sinus and its tributaries.

## 5. Stage interpretation in the app

| Stage | Teaching interpretation |
|---|---|
| CS10 | Early paired venous systems; no portal trunk layer displayed |
| CS12 | Paired vitelline conduits and hepatic development; portal trunk not yet displayed |
| CS13 | Human midgut-derived precursor and dorsal crossing shown as developing |
| CS14 | Portal branches become identifiable; human portal sinus shown as developing in the late-CS14 teaching bin |
| CS15 | Portal trunk, portal sinus and first-order portal branches shown as established / expanding |
| CS16 | Organized early fetal portal arrangement shown |

These bins are intentionally broad. The source papers do not establish every model state at an exact Carnegie-stage boundary. A rendered layer being hidden before its teaching bin is not proof of biological absence.

## 6. Left umbilical vein: scope note

A simplified left umbilical-vein path is included so learners can understand the portal sinus and the left-sided portal branches. It is not a full umbilical module:
- the right umbilical vein is not modeled;
- the complete paired prehepatic umbilical trunks are not modeled;
- placental and body-wall circulation are not modeled;
- the venous duct / ductus venosus is deferred to a later milestone.

Do not infer that the left umbilical vein is the only early umbilical channel from this limited teaching view.

## 7. Coordinate and geometry notes

The project coordinate convention remains:
- +X: embryonic right;
- −X: embryonic left;
- +Y: cranial;
- −Y: caudal;
- +Z: dorsal / posterior;
- −Z: ventral / anterior.

All vessel radii and centre-line coordinates are normalized schematic teaching geometry. They are not measured embryo diameters or a specimen-derived 3D reconstruction.
