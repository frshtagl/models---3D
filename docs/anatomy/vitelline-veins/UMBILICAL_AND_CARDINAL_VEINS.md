# Umbilical and Cardinal Veins — Developmental Anatomy Guide

## Scope

This guide explains the new paired umbilical and early cardinal modules. It distinguishes the persistent left umbilical vein from its early sinus-horn connection, and it compares the conventional segmental IVC model with the 2015 human reconstruction of the caudal cardinal and subcardinal systems.

## 1. Keep the three early venous systems distinct

- **Vitelline veins:** drain the yolk sac / developing gut and contribute to the portal and hepatic venous pathways.
- **Umbilical veins:** return placental blood to the embryo.
- **Cardinal veins:** drain the embryo proper.

The pathways meet near the venous pole, but their embryonic territories and derivatives are not interchangeable.

## 2. Paired umbilical veins

The early umbilical veins are paired. They connect the extraembryonic placental circulation to the venous pole through the developing liver region.

The app represents:
- the right umbilical vein as an early paired channel that becomes regressive and no longer distinct as a large conduit in later bins;
- the persistent left umbilical vein from placental end to hepatic hilum in the existing left-umbilical-inflow layer;
- the early left sinus-horn connection as a separate layer.

The early sinus-horn connection is not the whole left umbilical vein. Its remodeling must not be taught as regression of the persistent left umbilical vein.

### Postnatal fates

- Left umbilical vein → ligamentum teres hepatis (round ligament of the liver).
- Ductus venosus → ligamentum venosum.
- Right umbilical vein → regresses as a distinct vessel; it does not form the ligamentum teres.

The left umbilical vein and ductus venosus are different fetal vessels and form different remnants.

## 3. Cardinal venous system

### Anterior cardinal veins

Paired anterior cardinal veins drain the cranial part of the embryo. Their caudal ends join the caudal / posterior cardinal pathways.

### Common cardinal veins

The paired common cardinal veins (ducts of Cuvier) receive anterior and caudal / posterior cardinal return and enter the corresponding sinus venosus horns.

The head and neck drainage territories are not reconstructed in this milestone. The cranial ends are shown as positional anchors only.

### Caudal / posterior cardinal veins

The early paired caudal-body drainage channels are shown as caudal cardinal veins in the 2015 human reconstruction. Conventional textbooks commonly use posterior cardinal for the early paired system.

In the 2015 human embryo series, caudal cardinal veins extend caudally from the common cardinal region between CS11 and CS15. The model uses a source-specific name in the inspector and does not claim that the final infrarenal IVC is already formed at CS15 or CS16.

## 4. Subcardinal system and mesonephroi

Hikspoors et al. (2015) describe a venous plexus developing in and around the mesonephroi from CS15. The plexus is continuous with the caudal cardinal veins and develops ventromedially.

At CS16, longitudinal channels begin to form on the ventromedial sides of the mesonephroi. The study describes early intersubcardinal anastomoses just caudal to the superior mesenteric artery.

The app shows:
1. caudal cardinal veins;
2. a simplified mesonephric subcardinal plexus;
3. paired longitudinal subcardinal channels;
4. an early intersubcardinal connection.

This is not the completed adult renal collar. The later hepatosubcardinal junction and further IVC remodeling occur after the current CS16 timeline.

## 5. Two different IVC / azygos interpretations

### Conventional four-segment model

| Adult IVC region | Conventional embryonic contribution |
|---|---|
| Hepatic | Right vitelline / hepatocardiac channel |
| Suprarenal | Right subcardinal vein |
| Renal | Subcardinal–supracardinal anastomoses |
| Infrarenal | Right supracardinal vein |
| Common iliac veins | Posterior-cardinal derivatives |
| Thoracic azygos / hemiazygos | Thoracic supracardinal channels |

This is the familiar textbook segmental explanation. It is a useful model, but it is not the only human reconstruction interpretation.

### Hikspoors et al. (2015) human reconstruction

Their closely spaced human embryo reconstructions report:
- infrarenal IVC from the right caudal cardinal vein;
- renal region from the subcardinal system;
- azygos system from the cranial parts of the caudal cardinal veins;
- subcardinal plexus developing as a ventral outgrowth of the caudal cardinal system.

The authors argue that separate supracardinal / sacrocardinal systems are not required to explain their human reconstructions. The app therefore hides the conventional supracardinal layer when the human 2015 model is selected.

### Do not merge the two models

The conventional supracardinal channel is not an extra vessel to add to the 2015 human reconstruction. The model selector changes the interpretation and visible alternative layer. It does not blend the two lineages.

## 6. Stage interpretation

| Stage | Teaching interpretation |
|---|---|
| CS10 | Paired umbilical channels and early systemic venous return are shown as context |
| CS12 | Anterior, common and early caudal cardinal drainage are shown |
| CS13–CS14 | Right umbilical-vein regression is shown in broad teaching bins; left umbilical inflow remains |
| CS15 | Caudal cardinal extension and mesonephric subcardinal plexus are shown |
| CS16 | Longitudinal subcardinal channels and early intersubcardinal anastomosis are shown |

The human 2015 reconstruction continues through CS18–CS23 for the later IVC and azygos configuration. The current app does not yet animate those later stages.

## 7. Geometry and orientation notes

- +X: embryonic right.
- −X: embryonic left.
- +Y: cranial.
- −Y: caudal.
- +Z: dorsal / posterior.
- −Z: ventral / anterior.

The dorsal aorta and mesonephroi are positional context meshes. Vessel paths and radii are normalized schematic geometry, not specimen-derived measurements.
